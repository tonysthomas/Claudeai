# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The paying buyer is the right one.** SME owners, HR, L&D, compliance and executives in the UAE/GCC have budgets, sit in one reachable channel (LinkedIn), and the plan points to a growing market. Of the four market tests, money, reach and growth look plausible.
- **Governance is the only part of the offer that carries risk.** "Your staff already use AI without a policy, and that exposes you" is pain a budget-holder pays to remove. Fluency on its own is a nice-to-have.
- **Effort and time can be low.** Live workshops and starter kits ask little of the buyer, and the plan's own figure (instructor-led completion far above self-paced) favours done-with-you delivery.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nobody can see why it would work.** There are no case studies, no client results, no testimonials and no Phase 1 numbers. The certifications are self-issued, and the IAPP AIGP is the only outside credential. A corporate buyer paying $500–5,000 a seat has no proof to rely on, and on this lens weak likelihood is the most common reason an offer fails.
2. **It is a catalogue, not an offer.** It spans three pillars, five levels, five certifications, seven product types and two audiences. The masses don't pay and the payers aren't the masses, so the content engine feeds a $19 ebook while the money sits in workshops it does nothing to sell. No single offer is stacked, guaranteed or named around one outcome.
3. **The dream outcome is vague and easy to copy.** "AI Leadership" is not a result a CFO can measure. Plain-English AI fluency competes with free material and with what the AI vendors give away, so it slides toward a price fight. The plan's claim that governance is the gap in the market is unverified, because no competitor analysis has been run.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What measurable outcome does an SME get in 30 days: a signed AI policy, trained staff, an audit passed?
- Which UAE/GCC buyers are actually under governance pressure? The EU AI Act binds only those who serve the EU. Open question: run /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer.
- What are the actual Phase 1 results (posts, subscribers, saves) against the gate?
- What does one corporate client cost to win, and what is a workshop worth after delivery costs? Open question: /founder-cfo.
- Could you charge double for a governance sprint and still be the obvious choice? Why?
- What is the guarantee?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Narrow to one paid offer.** Make it a named governance offer for SMEs: a policy, a risk register and a staff training session, with a deliverable date and a guarantee (for example, a written AI policy in 30 days or the next session is free). Park levels 4–5, certifications and the cohort.
2. **Get proof before scale.** Deliver 3 paid or deeply discounted pilots with documented before-and-after results by the end of the Phase 3 window.
3. **Price the stack, not the ebook.** Treat the $19–27 products as lead magnets, and use /founder-cfo to confirm the workshop margin and the cost to win a client.
4. **Test the wedge.** Use /founder-competitors to confirm governance really is the gap, and /founder-consumer to confirm GCC buyers feel the pain.
